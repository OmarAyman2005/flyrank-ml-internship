Refresh Opportunity Scoring with Honest Temporal Validation

Can decision-time search and engagement signals rank content pages likely to decline in a later period, and can a learned model improve on a transparent rule-based baseline?

Lane: Refresh / Content Opportunity Scoring Model: Logistic Regression Development: Mar → Apr 2026 Sealed test: May → Jun 2026

1. Abstract

This capstone asks whether early, decision-time search and engagement signals can rank content pages that are likely to experience a meaningful decline in the following month. I build a public-safe page-level dataset from the FlyRank Internship warehouse, define decline as a greater-than-20% fall in average daily impressions, and compare a transparent CTR/visibility rule with Logistic Regression and Random Forest. On held-out clients, Logistic Regression achieved Precision@50 of 0.88 and Precision@100 of 0.76, clearly exceeding the baseline at 0.42 and 0.36. On the sealed May→June temporal test, the result was more nuanced: the baseline slightly led at Precision@50 (0.78 vs 0.76), while Logistic Regression led at Precision@100 (0.82 vs 0.71) and ROC AUC (0.6626 vs 0.5440). The final output is therefore a ranked human-review queue for refresh prioritization, framed as directional decision support rather than proof of causal refresh impact.

2. Introduction / Problem Statement

Content and SEO teams often have more pages than they can review manually. The practical decision is not merely whether a page may need attention, but which pages should be reviewed first when editorial capacity is limited.

The research question is: Can early, decision-time search and engagement signals identify and rank content pages likely to decline in a later period, and can a learned model outperform a transparent rule-based baseline on the same held-out data?

Decision supported: prioritize content pages for human refresh review. The model does not automatically prescribe a refresh; it ranks review candidates so an editor or SEO specialist can inspect intent, SERP context, and content quality before acting.

3. Data

The analysis uses the FlyRank Internship warehouse release on Hugging Face, specifically fact_content_daily_performance. One modeling row represents one anonymized content page identified internally by pseudonymized client and content keys.

The development design uses March 2026 as the feature window and April 2026 as the future outcome window. A separate May→June 2026 period is kept sealed until after the candidate model is selected. Because source availability differs across pages and months, impressions are normalized by the number of GSC-available days, and pages must have at least seven available days in both feature and outcome months.

The target proxy is fixed before model training: a page is labeled as declining when its next-month average daily impressions are below 80% of the feature-month average daily impressions. 4. Methodology

The task is binary classification used as a ranking problem: the classifier produces a probability of the future-decline proxy, and that probability becomes the review-priority score.

Features

  • feature-month impressions
  • feature-month clicks
  • feature-month average position
  • feature-month sessions
  • feature-month engaged sessions

Development evaluation uses a client-grouped train/test split. The training set contains 16,128 rows from 20 clients and the test set contains 1,879 rows from 7 different clients, with zero client overlap. This reduces leakage from client-specific patterns.

Three approaches are compared on the same held-out pages: a transparent rule baseline, Logistic Regression, and Random Forest. The rule combines search visibility with CTR underperformance relative to position buckets. Logistic Regression is selected as the final candidate after the held-out-client comparison; the feature set, decline threshold, baseline logic, model family, and metrics are then frozen before the May→June test.

Primary metric: Precision@50, because the operational question is “which pages should be reviewed first?” Precision@100 and ROC AUC are also reported so the very top of the queue and the broader ranking can both be evaluated.

6. Limitations & Honest Framing

This analysis is observational and should be interpreted as directional decision support, not as proof that any signal causes future decline or that refreshing a page will improve performance.

  • The warehouse is an unbalanced panel: clients have different history depth and analytics availability.
  • The seven-day availability filter improves comparability but changes which pages enter the modeling population.
  • The 20% decline threshold is a transparent operational proxy, not a universal measure of content quality.
  • Performance shifts across periods: the sealed test has a much higher decline base rate than the held-out-client development test.
  • Logistic Regression does not dominate the baseline at every cutoff; the baseline remains competitive for the very top of the queue.
What this work cannot claim: it does not prove Google’s algorithm, infer causal refresh impact, or justify automatic content changes without human review.
